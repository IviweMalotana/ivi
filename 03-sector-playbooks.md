## A. Law Firms

**What keeps them up at night:** billable hours lost to admin, missed deadlines (which are
malpractice risk), client intake friction, compliance (KYC/AML, conflict checks), and partners
doing R-thousands-an-hour work on tasks a workflow should handle.

**Where the money leaks:**
- Manual client intake & onboarding (forms, ID, conflict checks) taking days.
- KYC / AML / source-of-funds checks done by hand, inconsistently, with weak audit trails.
- Document drafting & review — repetitive contracts, NDAs, standard letters.
- Time capture leaking billable hours (work done, never recorded).
- Matter & deadline tracking living in someone's head or a spreadsheet.
- Billing and credit control — invoices late, WIP not converted, chasing done ad hoc.

**Automations you propose:**
| Play | What it does | Value |
|---|---|---|
| AI intake assistant | Collects client details, ID and matter info, runs conflict + AML checks, opens the matter | Days → minutes; consistent compliance |
| KYC/AML screening + audit trail | Identity, PEP, sanctions, adverse-media screening with a stored evidence trail | Regulatory safety; **you built exactly this in Emerald Path** |
| Document drafting/review copilot | Generates first-draft standard docs, flags risky clauses | Reclaims fee-earner hours |
| Deadline & matter tracker | Statutory/court deadlines derived automatically, escalated before they bite | Kills malpractice risk; **you built statutory-deadline tracking** |
| Billing & credit-control funnel | Auto-invoicing from WIP, structured chasing sequence | **You built a full credit-control funnel at bOnline** |

**Your proof to name-drop:** *"I built a compliance platform that runs live AML/PEP/sanctions
screening with a full audit trail, auto-derives statutory deadlines from filing dates, and
handles the whole document-generation pipeline. Your intake and KYC problem is the same shape."*

**Opening line for a managing partner:** *"How many billable hours a week do your fee-earners
lose to intake, compliance admin and chasing invoices? I make that number smaller with software
your team actually owns."*

## B. Finance / Fintech / Accountancy

**What keeps them up at night:** failed payments and bad debt, reconciliation eating the finance
team, fraud, month-end close dragging, and regulatory reporting. This is your home turf — lead
with it.

**Where the money leaks:**
- Failed / retried payments and involuntary churn (money simply not collected).
- Credit control & collections done inconsistently → rising bad debt.
- Bank/ledger reconciliation done manually every month.
- Invoice & document processing (AP/AR) by copy-paste.
- Fraud and compliance (KYC/AML) under-resourced or reactive.
- Month-end close and management reporting stitched together in spreadsheets.
- Statutory accounts & tax prep — slow, manual, error-prone.

**Automations you propose:**
| Play | What it does | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Failed-payment recovery engine | Smart retries, dunning sequences, direct-debit failure handling, reporting | **You built this and scaled billing 7× at bOnline** |
| Credit-control / dunning funnel | Structured, automated escalation to recover receivables | **Built at bOnline** — directly reduces bad debt |
| Reconciliation automation | Matches transactions to ledger, surfaces only the exceptions | Frees the finance team's month |
| Fraud / AML screening | Detection rules + identity/sanctions screening with audit trail | **You stood up bOnline's first fraud + AML process** |
| Statutory accounts & tax automation | Trial balance → adjustments → FRS-102 accounts → CT/VAT/SA → filing hand-off | **This is literally Emerald Path** |
| Month-end / reporting automation | Auto-built management packs from source systems | Days of analyst time back |

**Your proof to name-drop:** *"At a UK fintech I owned payments, billing and fraud — I built the
direct-debit failure system, the credit-control funnel, and the company's first fraud/AML
process, and scaled billing 7× without adding headcount. Then I built Emerald Path, a platform

**Opening line for a CFO:** *"What percentage of your revenue quietly never gets collected —

## C. Insurance

**What keeps them up at night:** claims cost and cycle time, fraud leakage, underwriting
consistency, and a mountain of documents (policies, claims forms, medical/loss evidence) handled
manually.

**Where the money leaks:**
- Claims intake & triage — slow, manual, inconsistent routing.
- Document extraction from claims/policy/medical PDFs by hand.
- Fraud detection late or reactive (paid before it's caught).
- Underwriting inconsistency — decisions varying by who's on shift.
- Policy administration & endorsements done manually.
- Customer service volume for status-chasing and simple queries.

**Automations you propose:**
| Play | What it does | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Claims intake & triage assistant | Captures a claim, classifies severity, routes it, requests missing info | Faster cycle time, lower cost-per-claim |
| Document extraction pipeline | Pulls structured data from claims/policy/medical documents | Kills rekeying; **your Emerald Path parses financial docs at scale** |
| Fraud-signal detection | Flags suspicious claims for review before payout | Direct loss reduction; **you built fraud detection in fintech** |
| Underwriting copilot | Consistent, rules-guided assessments with human sign-off | Consistency + speed; mirrors your **AI-proposes-human-approves** pattern |
| Policyholder AI assistant | Deflects status/simple queries, escalates the rest | **You ran an AI chatbot to 60% resolution + an AI receptionist** |

**Your proof to name-drop:** *"I've worked inside an insurance/fintech product environment, and
the AI systems I've shipped follow the pattern regulators like — the AI proposes and a human
approves, with a full audit trail. That's exactly how claims triage and underwriting assistance
should work."*

**Opening line for a Head of Claims / COO:** *"Where in your claims journey are you paying people
to retype what's already written on a document — or paying out on claims you'd have caught with a
second look? Both are automatable, and I've built the pieces before."*

## The through-line across all three sectors

All three are **document-heavy, deadline-driven, compliance-bound, and money-sensitive** — and
the automation pattern is the same one you've already proven:

> **Ingest the messy input → let AI propose → let a human approve → keep a full audit trail →
> report the money saved.**

That's the Emerald Path pattern (AI-assisted, human-approved, deterministic, audited). Your
pitch is that you've *already built the reference implementation* — now you're installing it
where it's needed. That's a much stronger story than "I know some AI tools."
