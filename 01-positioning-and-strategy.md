## 2. Why this positioning — and why you specifically

Most people selling "AI" are selling a *tool*. You are selling an *outcome*, and you have an
unusual, credible right to do it. Three things make you different, and the whole brand leans on
them:

**(a) You are an engineer who learned to run the business, not a consultant who learned to
spell "API."**
BSc Electrical & Computer Engineering (UCT). You've written the Python, wired the webhooks,
deployed the Lambdas *and* owned the P&L conversation with the CFO. You can sit with the
engineers and the executives in the same afternoon and be trusted by both. That's rare and it's
the core of your premium.

**(b) You have already done this across the exact three sectors you want to sell into.**
- **Finance** — payments, billing, credit control, fraud and reconciliation at bOnline (UK
  fintech). You *scaled billing 7×* and stood up the company's first fraud/AML process.
- **Insurance** — a health-insurance product and financial-management tooling at LifeCheq.
- **Legal / regulated compliance** — Emerald Path automates statutory accounts, Corporation
  Tax, VAT, Self-Assessment, AML/KYC and Companies House filing: the exact document-heavy,
  deadline-driven, compliance-bound work that law firms and accountancies drown in.

You are not *claiming* you could work in these sectors. You have shipped in them.

**(c) You build the whole thing, not a slide about it.**
A whole white-label platform, built and shipped end to end — with a 253-test regression suite
guarding live tax calculations, proper tenant isolation, disaster recovery and monitoring, and
commercialised as a real venture. When you say "I'll automate that," you can actually hand over
working software. That converts sceptical buyers.

**(d) You are AI-native — you automate your own delivery.**
You don't just talk about AI; you build *with* it. You orchestrate a fleet of AI coding agents —
**Claude Code, OpenAI Codex and Cowork** — through a structured, human-gated pipeline (plan →
build → test → review), verify real user journeys **end-to-end with Playwright**, and ship on a
**token-based design system** with **mobile-first engineering**. This is the meta-flex: you
automate your *own* work, so clients get more automation, faster and cheaper — and it holds up in
production because it was tested and designed, not hacked together.

**The synthesis:** *"An engineer-operator who has already automated the money, the risk and the
paperwork in finance, insurance and compliance — and can prove it with shipped product."*

## 3. The brand persona — translating "very Rihanna" into a professional brand

"Very Rihanna" is a real strategic instruction, not a joke. Here's what it means for the brand
and how it shows up:

| Rihanna trait | Your professional translation |
|---|---|
| Multi-hyphenate — singer *and* Fenty mogul | Engineer *and* product owner *and* founder (Victor The Label). You contain multiple businesses; you don't apologise for it. |
| Owns her narrative, never over-explains | Confident, plain-spoken copy. Short sentences. No jargon-stacking to sound smart. |
| Fenty = "clean, inclusive, premium, effortless" | Your visual brand: **clean, white, minimal, light-grey, expensive-feeling through restraint** — not loud. |
| Makes bold moves quietly, then everyone catches up | You lead with proof and outcomes, let the work be the flex. |
| Unmistakably herself | One name. **Ivi Malotana.** Not "Ivi Malotana Consulting Solutions Ltd." Just the name. |

**Your signature brand device** — the objection you've heard a hundred times, flipped:

> *"Ivi, we can't automate this. It needs human intervention."*
> — what teams say right before you automate it. (That "impossible" accountant now has six extra
> hours a week.)

Put it on the site, in decks, in talks. It names the objection and demolishes it in one breath —
and it proves the positioning: you find automation where everyone else swears there isn't any.

**Brand voice rules:**
- Speak in outcomes and money, not features. "I cut your failed-payment losses," not "I
  implement a retry orchestration layer."
- Confidence without arrogance. State what you've done as fact; don't hedge it to death.
- Plain English. If a CFO or a managing partner can't follow it, rewrite it.
- Short. Rihanna doesn't pad.

## 4. Visual identity (clean, white, minimalist — light greys)

*Per your direction: very clean and minimalist. The premium comes from whitespace and
restraint, not colour.*

**Palette**
| Role | Colour | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Ink (primary text) | Near-black | `#111214` |
| Paper (background) | White | `#FFFFFF` |
| Mist (light grey panels) | Light grey | `#F4F5F6` |
| Line (hairlines, borders) | Grey | `#E3E5E8` |
| Slate (secondary text) | Mid grey | `#6B7075` |
| Accent (used *sparingly* — one hairline or dot) | Graphite | `#2B2D31` |

No bright colours. The accent is just a slightly warmer/darker grey. Monochrome discipline is
the look.

**Type**
- Headlines: a clean grotesque — **Inter**, **Neue Haas / Helvetica Now**, or **DM Sans** (you
  already standardised the Dixon Hub on DM Sans — stay consistent). Tight letter-spacing on big
  headings.
- Body: same family, regular weight, generous line-height.
- One trick for the "editorial" feel: set your name in a large, thin weight with wide spacing —
  `I V I   M A L O T A N A`.

**Layout language**
- Lots of white space. Never fill the page.
- Hairline rules (1px light grey) to divide, never heavy boxes.
- Left-aligned, single accent line, everything breathing.

**Logo / wordmark**
- Just the name set cleanly: **Ivi Malotana**, with the role beneath in small tracked caps:
  `AI & AUTOMATION SPECIALIST`.
- Optional monogram for avatars/favicon: **IM** or **iv.** in the same clean face.

## 5. Who you sell to (Ideal Customer Profile)

You are selling into **operations-heavy, compliance-bound, mid-sized businesses** where manual
process is a visible cost. Three tiers:

**Primary buyers (who signs the cheque):**
- **Law firms:** Managing Partner, COO / Practice Manager, Head of Operations.
- **Finance / fintech / accountancy:** CFO, Finance Director, Head of Operations, COO.
- **Insurance:** COO, Head of Claims, Head of Underwriting Operations, Head of Digital.

**Company shape that's your sweet spot:**
- 20–500 people (big enough to have painful process, small enough to move without a 12-month
  procurement cycle).
- Regulated or compliance-heavy (they *have* to keep records, hit deadlines, screen clients).
- Still doing meaningful work in spreadsheets, email threads, and copy-paste between systems.

**The trigger events that mean "call Ivi now":**
- Scaling and drowning — headcount rising just to keep up with volume.
- A near-miss on compliance / a missed deadline / a fraud or bad-debt spike.
- A new system going in (CRM, practice-management, policy-admin) and nobody has time to wire it
  up properly.
- A leader who just read something about AI and is under pressure to "do something."

## 6. The offer ladder (detailed in `02-services-and-pricing.md`)

The brand hooks people with the *diagnosis* and keeps them with the *build*:

1. **The Automation Opportunity Scan** — the hook. A fixed-fee audit where you walk the
   business and hand back a ranked map of what to automate and what it's worth. This *is* your
   "I can see the opportunities" promise, productised.
2. **The Automation Sprint** — a fixed-scope build of the highest-value automation, in weeks.
3. **The AI Enablement Retainer** — ongoing, you become their outsourced automation function.
4. **Fractional Head of Automation** — embedded, part-time leadership for a company going all-in.
